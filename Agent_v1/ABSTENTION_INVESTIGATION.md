# DataMate abstention investigation

## What the runs establish

| Evidence | Observed outcome | Cause supported by the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Original Kaggle log | Multiple businesses failed before answering questions | Model-generated mappings requested deduplication without an order identity. The business preparation step rejected the mapping. |
| Early 20-question Kaggle run | 20 abstentions, 40 failed attempts | Every planning attempt reported CUDA out of memory. |
| Later Kaggle batch | 15 abstentions and two numeric responses among the 17 question outcomes printed in the excerpt | The 30 warnings comprise 14 execution, 10 review, four serialization, one validation and one planning failure. These are attempt counts. |
| v1.0.4 CPU audit, training rows 21–1000 | 132 correct, zero wrong, 848 abstentions | Qwen was disabled. The verified route did not support the intent for 680 questions. The remaining 168 abstentions involved evidence or mapping gaps. |
| v1.0.4 CPU audit, all 1,000 public questions | 14 answers, 986 abstentions | Qwen was disabled. The verified route did not support 920 question intents. The public split has no released references. |

The training audit covers 30 businesses and the public audit covers 14. Cell 8 filters questions by business before answering them. Cell 10 groups the entire split by business and prepares each group's data. The business filter therefore cannot account for an abstention on a selected question.

Only 16 of the 980 training references are labeled `unanswerable`. The reference type represents a supported finding that the data cannot answer the question. An `abstain` scores zero. A wrong guess scores −1 and a correct answer scores +1 under the supplied competition overview.

## Failure paths in the current code

1. **Coverage of documented queries.** The verified route accepts only recognized intents with adequate schema and data evidence. In the regular pipeline, the remaining questions move to Qwen. The CPU audit measures this route's coverage and establishes how much the regular run depends on Qwen.
2. **SQL grounding and result shape.** Real failures included missing or ambiguous columns, invalid date bindings, excessive result rows and incorrect scalar or list projections. The previous repair prompt treated a scalar answer failure like a verification-check failure. This investigation corrected the repair instructions for both categories and added targeted feedback for the observed binder, list and row-cap errors. Several other logged errors, including extra parameters, literal date placeholders and some stale source configurations, were already addressed in v1.0.4.
3. **Semantic review.** A correct-looking query may still use an incorrect business rule. The reviewer caught issues such as an omitted shift filter, and the logs also show false objections to valid date windows and malformed JSON. The current pipeline resets a candidate to abstain when review fails and the repair budget is exhausted. Removing review outright could increase wrong answers.
4. **Time budget.** The later excerpt shows a median of 238.8 seconds per printed outcome and a total of about 62 minutes for 17 outcomes. A five-hour Kaggle configuration allows at most 18 seconds per question over 1,000 questions before model loading, business preparation and finalization. The 17 questions are a small historical batch. This comparison identifies a throughput risk rather than predicting a current runtime. The journal exports unfinished questions as abstain placeholders. The diagnostic script now reports unfinished questions separately from committed outcomes, counts failed attempts by stage, and reports recorded question time.

## Pipeline solution and how to verify it

1. **Establish a GPU baseline.** Run the current agent with the official local checkpoint on a fixed labeled sample drawn across businesses, output types and question families. Include verified-route misses and several businesses that were outside the original development batch. Save the run's `selection.json`, `results/`, `logs/`, and `progress.json`. Track completed coverage, correct and wrong counts, net PA, abstentions by final stage, attempted failure counts, and time spent in setup and answering. Keep a separate set of whole businesses for validation.
2. **Constrain the model's plan and test it against the catalog.** Benchmark a smaller structured plan containing the query, answer shape and rule references. JSON Schema constrained generation is available in vLLM. Validate compatibility with the checkpoint, offline container and GPU before switching backends. Keep deterministic SQL checks for table names, bound dates, result shape and read-only execution. Feed specific binder, execution and output failures back into a bounded repair attempt. This applies execution-guided refinement at the point where the pipeline can observe the actual failure. [vLLM structured outputs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/structured_outputs/) · [Execution-guided decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03100) · [Execution-guided refinement](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.120/)
3. **Make review depend on the evidence and the risk of the query.** Evaluate a policy that checks periods, cardinality, required fields and output shape deterministically. Use model review for remaining questions about business semantics, including late returns, historical stock, shifts, units and missing-data claims. Require review objections to point to a specific predicate or source fact. Measure correct candidates that were rejected and incorrect candidates that were accepted using held-out training references. Keep explicit `unanswerable` for substantiated absence.
4. **Expand verified SQL where it pays off across businesses.** Rank uncovered question families by frequency on training data. Add reusable query logic only where the business schema and rule evidence support it. Validate across held-out businesses. Keep public and private data out of training, and keep inference offline under the competition's model and compute limits.
5. **Validate the full pipeline before judging the fix.** Compare the candidate and baseline on the same held-out training questions. Require positive net PA improvement, lower avoidable abstention, and report any increase in wrong answers. Then run all public IDs to completion and check failures and runtime. Public results alone establish completion and coverage. The supplied competition rules also require the final offline image to finish on the reference hardware within six hours and to meet the reproducibility tolerance.

The attached Kaggle excerpts establish historical failure modes. The updated Qwen path and full-run throughput still require a GPU run with the checkpoint. The local workspace has a 4 GB GPU and no checkpoint. The repairs and diagnostics added here do not establish a new public-test score.
